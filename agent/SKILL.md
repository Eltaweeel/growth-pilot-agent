# Growth Experimentation Playbook

Use this skill when a customer asks for a growth audit, funnel analysis, campaign plan, experiment backlog, KPI framework, or growth automation design.

## Method
1. Clarify outcome, customer, offer, market, time horizon, constraints, and decision.
2. Inventory evidence. Label each item as observed, customer-stated, public-source, inferred, hypothesis, or synthetic.
3. Define funnel stages and conversion events. Use AARRR when it helps locate the bottleneck; ask the customer whether they want to use it.
4. Ask the customer to define their North Star Metric. If unclear, explain selection criteria and propose candidate metric(s) with formula, rationale, and guardrails; let the customer choose.
5. Find opportunities grounded in friction, customer value, economics, or operational waste. State alternative explanations.
6. Prioritize with transparent ICE/RICE only as a decision aid. Scores are relative judgments, not objective forecasts.
7. Turn priorities into experiment cards with hypothesis, audience, intervention/comparison, metric, guardrails, feasibility, timebox, decision rule, owner, cost ceiling, and approval needs.
8. Add campaign briefs/content drafts that match evidence, offer, voice, market, channel, and substantiation requirements.
9. Identify automation candidates. Specify trigger, inputs, transformations, outputs, exception handling, approvals, logs, monitoring, owner, and rollback.
10. Close with measurement plan, uncertainty, and the next decision.

## Metric hygiene
- Write formulas and units. Confirm denominator, timeframe, timezone, currency, channel attribution, and source.
- Separate leads from qualified leads; inquiries from bookings; gross revenue from contribution profit; platform ROAS from incremental return.
- Do not compare incompatible attribution windows or platforms without caveat.
- Avoid North Star metrics that reward activity without customer value or profitable business impact. Add guardrails for margin, refunds, quality, capacity, and customer experience where relevant.

## Experiment integrity
- Do not call an observational before/after comparison a controlled A/B test.
- State whether assignment is randomized, what is controlled, and what confounds remain.
- Avoid declaring a winner from tiny samples or noisy short windows. If evidence is weak, report inconclusive and recommend a better learning design.
- Never fabricate results. Hypothetical ranges must be labeled projections with assumptions; synthetic data must be labeled at every point of use.

## Market adaptation
- Ask which country/city, language, segment, season, and channel matter. Egypt/MENA knowledge is a hypothesis generator, not a substitute for local evidence.
- Respect local culture and advertising rules. Avoid broad claims about national behavior without credible support.
- Follow the customer's language. For Arabic, clarify Egyptian colloquial versus Modern Standard Arabic where relevant.

## Automation-first review
Prioritize automations that are repetitive, measurable, reversible, and bounded. Keep human approval for publication, outbound communication, money/budget changes, sensitive data, and irreversible actions. Account for failure alerts, duplicate prevention, access scope, and auditability.
